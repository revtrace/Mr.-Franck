Monaco is close enough to Nice Airport that people assume it's simple. Then they arrive with luggage at eleven at night and discover the principality is a warren of one-way tunnels, underground car parks and addresses that satnavs place on the wrong level.

Franck does this run constantly. He knows which entrance your hotel actually uses, which tunnel to take at the Casino, and that the lift from the Port Hercule car park comes out two streets from where you thought it would.

What the journey looks like

  • Met inside the arrivals hall with your name on a sign — not in a car park.
  • Your flight tracked, so a delay costs nothing and changes nothing.
  • The A8 autoroute when time matters; the Moyenne Corniche when you'd rather see the coast. Franck will ask.
  • Dropped at the door — the Hermitage, the Fairmont, Port Hercule, the Casino, or a private address in Fontvieille.
  • No border formality. Monaco is a separate country, but there is nothing to show and nothing to stop for.

How else could you do it?

Honestly, including where the alternatives are the better call.

Taxi from the rank

Metered, with airport and luggage supplements you learn about at the end. The fare to Monaco is not small, and you will not know it until you arrive.

Tram, then train

Cheap and genuinely scenic. It also means a tram from the airport into Nice-Ville, a wait, a train, and then the long walk up out of Monaco's station with your cases. Fine unencumbered on a sunny afternoon; less so at midnight with a family.

Airport bus

Cheapest of all, and slow. Fixed stops, limited luggage space, and it stops running earlier than most flights land.
