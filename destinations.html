Routes & destinations

Everything worth seeing is within an hour.

That's the quiet advantage of this coast. Monaco, Cannes, Èze and Antibes are all a short drive from Nice — and Provence and Italy are a comfortable day out. Here is what's where, how long it really takes, and what to do when you arrive.

Where to spend the day

Pick one, mix two, or tell Franck what kind of day you want and let him build it.

35–50 min from Nice

Monaco & Monte-Carlo

The Casino square, the Prince's Palace and the changing of the guard at noon, Port Hercule, and the Grand Prix circuit you can drive on any ordinary Tuesday.
30–40 min from Nice

Èze & the Corniches

A medieval village welded to a cliff 400 metres above the sea, and the three corniche roads — the best twenty minutes of driving on this coast.
30–40 min from Nice

Cannes

La Croisette, the Palais des Festivals and the red carpet, the old town at Le Suquet, and the boat across to the Îles de Lérins.
25–35 min from Nice

Antibes & Cap d'Antibes

The Provençal market, the ramparts, the Picasso museum, and the largest superyachts in the Mediterranean tied up at Port Vauban.
25–35 min from Nice

Villefranche & Cap Ferrat

Ochre houses stacked around one of the deepest natural harbours in the Med, and the Villa Ephrussi de Rothschild with its gardens on the cape.
1 h 30–2 h 30 from Nice

Saint-Tropez

The old port, the Place des Lices market on Tuesday and Saturday, Pampelonne beach. Worth the drive — and much better with someone else doing it.
Full day

Provence & the vineyards

Côtes de Provence estates, Grasse and its perfume houses, hilltop villages, and lavender in June and July. A long lunch is compulsory.
1 h+ from Nice

The Italian Riviera

Ventimiglia's Friday market, Bordighera, San Remo and its casino — or a full day west to Portofino. Bring ID; lunch is the reason you came.
30–45 min from Nice

Saint-Paul-de-Vence

A walled village of galleries and cobbled lanes, the Fondation Maeght, and Chagall's grave in the cemetery at the top. Go early or go late.
